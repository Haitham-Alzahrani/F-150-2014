**The gain is not a peaky one.** +12.2 % at 4665 rpm against +10.2 % at the peak
means the curve lifted through the midrange, not just at one point.

**Peak power moved UP 400 rpm and peak torque moved DOWN 110 rpm.** The engine is
being allowed to pull further before the calibration backs it off.

## The air/fuel ratio — the reading worth checking, and its source is UNKNOWN

The lower panel is labelled **`Air/Fuel Ratio 1`**. At the 4665 rpm cursor it
calls out **14.84 (red, before)** and **12.78 (blue, after)**.

**If that is a tailpipe wideband, it is the most important thing on the screen.**
12.78 is correct for wide open throttle on a naturally aspirated engine. **14.84
at full load is dangerously lean** — it makes less power and invites detonation,
and it would be a real fault in the original calibration.

**DO NOT ACT ON THIS YET. The source has not been established.** WinPEP takes
air/fuel from a dyno-mounted wideband OR from the OBD port, and the two mean
completely different things here:

* **Dyno wideband** → an **independent** measurement, and it **contradicts this
  project's own record**, which has `docs/f150-diagnosis.md` and `CLAUDE.md`
  stating *"12.3:1 commanded and delivered at wide throttle"* from the truck's
  own sensor. Two sensors disagreeing is exactly the lying-sensor evidence
  `CLAUDE.md` says has never been captured.
* **Read from the OBD port** → not independent at all, and it says only that the
  tune changed what the PCM commands.

**Ask the shop which it was.** It costs one question and it decides whether this
is a major finding or a footnote.

## 227 hp at the wheels — what it does and does not say

Against the **302 hp** crank rating, 227.16 is **75 %** — a 25 % loss, rising to
**83 %** after the tune. A 4x4 with a transfer case loses more than a 4x2, and
whether it ran in two or four wheel drive is not recorded on this screen.
**Both numbers are consistent with an ordinary driveline loss and neither
establishes a power deficit.**

**But it retires an estimate this project has been quoting as though it were a
measurement.** `CLAUDE.md` says *"215 g/s × ~1.4 ≈ 301 hp against a 302 hp
rating"* — that is airflow arithmetic with a rule-of-thumb multiplier, not a
measurement, and there is now a real dynamometer number. They do not conflict
(crank versus wheel) but **the airflow figure must stop being cited as evidence
the engine makes its rated power.**

## WHAT THIS CHANGES FOR THE INVESTIGATION — read before quoting any older figure

### The calibration is no longer stock. That splits the whole project.

`CLAUDE.md` states in three places that this truck has **"no aftermarket tune"**
and lists **"PCM tune"** among the items eliminated. **Both are now out of date.**
Shift points and throttle response were deliberately changed on 2026-09-16.

**Every measurement in this repository was taken on the previous calibration.**
The idle governor gain, the 0.33 Hz oscillation, the spark authority of ±0.9°,
the trims, the inferred ethanol value, the first-order content in engine speed —
all of it is now a **pre-tune** baseline. It stays valid as history. **It is no
longer a valid comparison for anything captured from today onward** unless the
capture is explicitly labelled post-tune.

**`CLAUDE.md` wanted a calibration change and named it the only legitimate lever
on this idle:** *"A Ford calibration update, if one exists for this PCM. That is
the only legitimate lever on dither amplitude and governor gain."* **Something in
that family has now happened, by a different route.** Whether it touched idle at
all is unknown and is directly testable.

### The throttle change touches a standing complaint

The owner's own words, carried in `CLAUDE.md`: *"it is hard for me to adjust the
RPM at 1000 because something is working on behalf of me of adjusting the RPM."*
**That is a pedal-to-plate complaint on a drive-by-wire throttle, and the
pedal-to-plate mapping is exactly what was just changed.** Worth asking him
directly whether holding 1000 and 1500 rpm feels different now.

### The Park idle shake should NOT be expected to change

Shift points do not apply in Park. A throttle map does not apply at a closed
throttle. **Neither change targets the felt shake, and if it is unchanged
tomorrow that is expected, not a disappointment.**

### The sensor swap experiment SURVIVES — but it needs one thing confirmed

The pending experiment asks **which side** needs more fuel after all four oxygen
sensors were moved across. **A calibration change applies to both banks equally,
so a bank-versus-bank difference is not destroyed by it.** The experiment is
still live and still decisive.

**What WOULD destroy it is an adaptive memory reset.** Many tuners disconnect the
battery or run a reset as part of flashing. `CLAUDE.md`'s standing rule —
*"never wipe the adaptive memory before a measurement unless the wipe is the
experiment"* — has now been at risk twice in three days without anyone asking.
**Establish it before reading any trim.**

## What to ask for, in order

1. **Did the tuner disconnect the battery or reset the adaptive memory?**
   Decides whether the pending trim capture has a baseline.
2. **Is `Air/Fuel Ratio 1` from a dyno wideband or from the OBD port?**
   Decides whether 14.84 versus the truck's own 12.3 is a real sensor conflict.
3. **The calibration identifier**, now that it has certainly changed.
   `f150diag survey` prints it, and `CLAUDE.md` has wanted it since the start.
4. **The four `.wp8` run files, or a printout of all four.** Runs 1 and 2 have
   never been seen, and the full traces carry far more than two peak numbers.
5. **Whether an OBD log was recorded during the pulls.** A dyno pull at full
   throttle IS outstanding capture #3 — `Oxygen sensor 1 Wide Range Equivalence
   ratio` against `Oxygen sensor 5 Wide Range Equivalence ratio`, the test that
   separates a driver-side air leak from a lean-biased sensor. If the shop logged
   it, that capture is already taken.
