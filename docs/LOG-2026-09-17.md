# THE 108-MINUTE LOG, 2026-09-17 15:49 — post-tune baseline, and a tool bug

**`data/carscanner/2026-09-17-full-page/2026-09-17_15-49-53.csv`** — 14 MB,
121,307 rows, 74 channels, **108 minutes stationary** (`Vehicle speed` 0.00 in
all 596 samples), idle 652 rpm.

## FIRST: A BUG IN MY OWN TOOLS, CAUGHT HERE, AND IT CHANGED A HEADLINE

`data/rpm_rate.py` and `data/idle_events.py` both resample onto a uniform grid
with `np.interp`. **`np.interp` draws a straight line across any hole in the
source, and a straight line has almost no rate of change and produces no events.**

This log has a **47.5-minute gap** in `Engine RPM`. **44 % of the grid fell
inside it.** The rate-of-change tool reported **1.82 rpm/s** — which would have
been a spectacular result, four times quieter than the healthy truck. **On real
coverage only it is 12.50 rpm/s.**

Both tools now discard any grid point that spans a gap over 1 s. **Every session
was recomputed.**

### What the fix did to the standing finding

| | Before the fix | **After** |
|---|---|---|
| 2014 median rate of change | 8.37 rpm/s | **13.53 rpm/s** |
| 2023 control | 7.17 | 7.17 |
| **Ratio** | **1.17×** | **1.89×** |

`CLAUDE.md` said *"on peak-to-peak span the 2014 is 1.90× the control; on rate of
change it is 1.17× — two metrics, same data, very different verdicts."*

**That disagreement WAS THE BUG.** Corrected, rate of change gives **1.89×**
against peak-to-peak's **1.90×**. **The two metrics agree almost exactly.** The
whole "which metric tracks what he feels" puzzle dissolves — they are measuring
the same thing.

## THE COMPRESSOR STATE IS KNOWN FOR THE FIRST TIME

`CLAUDE.md` has said for weeks that **every amplitude figure in the file must
state whether the compressor was running, and none could.** `[PCM] A/C Pressure`
answers here — **1070 to 1518 kPa, 7,621 samples, cycling with a dominant period
of 49.4 s.**

**That is not the 15.78 s cycle recorded in 2026-09.** Different cycle rate,
different day, different ambient — recorded, not explained.

**The comparison that matters:** the only previous session with the compressor
known to be cycling gave a **69 rpm** median span. **Today, with it cycling,
31.0 rpm.** Conditions are not matched and the two numbers come from different
methods, so this is not a clean before-and-after — but it is the first time the
compressor has been *measured* rather than guessed at.

## WHAT THE CAPTURE FAILED TO DO, AND WHY

**74 channels were on the page.** The tiles law did the rest.

| Channel asked for | Samples | Usable? |
|---|---|---|
| `[PCM] Currently Detected Engine Misfire` | **1** | **no** |
| `[PCM] Knock Sensor 1` / `2` | **2** / **1** | **no** |
| `Timing advance` | **2** | **no** |
| `Short term fuel % trim - Bank 1` | 4,663 | yes, but see below |

**And the worst of it: `Engine RPM` has a 47.5-minute hole (58028 → 60877) that
covers the ENTIRE window in which all six cylinder channels were polled.**

All six cylinders DO overlap, for 10.8 minutes, ~591 samples each — **the window
this project has wanted since night one.** But engine speed is absent from every
second of it, so **the engine's condition during that window is unknown, which is
exactly the flaw that invalidated the previous per-cylinder reading.** No cylinder
channel overlaps engine speed anywhere in this log.

**The bank comparison also failed:** in the 60-minute block where engine speed
exists, `Short term fuel % trim - Bank 1` has **1 sample**. Bank 2 has 7,838.

## THE LESSON, STATED AS A RULE

**Putting everything on the page does not capture everything. It captures a
little of a lot, badly, and the app decides which.** Seventy-four tiles produced
one misfire sample and a 47-minute hole in the single most important channel.

**Two tiles. Three when engine speed is only there to prove the condition.
Never more.**
